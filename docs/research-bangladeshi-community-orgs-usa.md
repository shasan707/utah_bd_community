## 2. How the Money System Works

### 2.1 Legal structure (the foundation everything sits on)

- Nearly every serious org registers as a **501(c)(3) nonprofit corporation** in its state.
  - Donations become **tax-deductible** for donors (a major selling point on donate pages).
  - The org opens a **bank account in the organization's name** (never a personal account).
  - Must file **IRS Form 990** annually; finances are semi-public (see GuideStar/Cause IQ profiles).
- Governance: an elected **Executive Committee (EC)** — President, General Secretary, **Treasurer**, plus secretaries (Cultural, Sports, Publicity, Social Welfare, Education...). Bangladesh Society NYC runs a 19-member EC with a separate **Election Commission** for credible elections.
- **Paid membership = voting rights** in EC elections. This is the engine of renewals: people pay dues to vote and to be eligible to run for the committee.
- The Treasurer keeps the books and presents a **financial report at the Annual General Meeting (AGM)**. Visible transparency is what keeps the community trusting the committee with money.

### 2.2 Income stream #1 — Membership dues (real numbers)

| Organization | Tiers & prices | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| COBCA (Columbus, OH) | Student $10 · Student family $15 · Individual $30 · Family $40 per year | Low-cost Midwest model. |
| BACA (NYC) | General $20/yr · **Lifetime $200 one-time** | Lifetime tier = upfront capital. |
| Bangla-O-Biswa (Boston) | Individual $215 · Family $375 (2 adults+2 kids) · Patron $500 · Grand Patron $600 per year | Premium model: dues bundle free admission to 4 flagship events (Durga Puja, Saraswati Puja, Basanta Utsav, Annual Picnic), priority performance slots, discounted vendor tables, voting rights; higher tiers get premium seating, grandparent coverage, free website ads. PayPal checkout. |
| Bangladesh Society (NYC) | Life members + General members | Fees not published; also sells member benefits like burial plots at cost. |

- Membership year commonly runs **July 1 – June 30**; fees may be adjusted annually based on budget.
- Standard perks pattern: **free/discounted flagship-event admission + voting rights + performance/vendor priority**.

### 2.3 Income stream #2 — Event revenue

- **Ticketed events:** concerts with artists flown from Bangladesh, puja dinners, gala nights. Sold via PayPal links, Eventbrite/desieventsgo-style platforms, or at the door.
- **Flagship melas (Boishakhi Mela, Victory Day, Eid programs) are usually FREE** — sponsors cover them; some charge ~$5 at the door / free with registration.
- **Vendor/stall fees:** food stalls, clothing/jewelry vendors, and businesses pay booth fees at melas.

### 2.4 Income stream #3 — Sponsorships (the biggest lever for large events)

| Level (FOBANA 2023 Dallas) | Price |
|---|---|
| Grand Sponsor | $30,000 |
| Icon Sponsor | $25,000 |
| Diamond Sponsor | $20,000 |
| Platinum Sponsor | $15,000 |
| Gold Sponsor | $5,000 |
| Silver Sponsor | $1,500 |

- Earlier/smaller conventions: Title $15,000 · Grand $6,000 · Platinum $4,000 · Gold $3,000 · Silver $2,000.
- Local org events typically run **$500–$5,000** tiers.
- **Who sponsors:** community businesses — realtors, restaurants, travel agencies, remittance companies, groceries, law/tax offices, clinics.
- **What they get:** logo on website & stage banners, ads in the printed **souvenir magazine (smaranika)**, stage mentions, free stalls, VIP seats.

### 2.5 Income stream #4 — Donations & designated giving

- General donation buttons (PayPal) on almost every site.
- **Designated giving** (Bangladesh Society model): donor picks education / summer camp / clean water / community welfare; supports one-time, weekly, monthly, yearly, or lifetime contribution schedules.
- Disaster-relief drives for Bangladesh (floods/cyclones) — periodic surge fundraising.
- Scholarship funds (FOBANA: ~$100k distributed since 2015).

### 2.6 Payment rails actually used

| Method | Usage pattern |
|---|---|
| **Zelle → treasurer's email** | Dominant, fee-free. BCS Cleveland publishes `treasurer@bcscle.org`; payer writes name + event/membership in the memo. Treasurer reconciles manually. |
| **PayPal buttons/links** | The standard "Donate" and membership checkout on most sites (Bangla-O-Biswa, BASGH, Poorba). ~3% fees. |
| Cash / checks | Still common at events and for older members. |
| Stripe / Eventbrite / Zeffy / Ticketor | Larger orgs, for real ticketing. Zeffy is popular with nonprofits (0% platform fee). |

### 2.7 The circulation cycle (how money flows through the year)

```
 Membership dues + sponsorships collected early in membership year (≈ July)
        │
        ▼
 Fund FREE flagship events (Boishakhi Mela, Victory Day, Eid, Independence Day)
        │
        ▼
 Ticketed concerts + vendor booth fees + souvenir-magazine ads generate SURPLUS
        │
        ▼
 Surplus → scholarships, charity/relief drives, equipment, seed money for next year
        │
        ▼
 Treasurer presents financial report at the AGM → members see where money went
        │
        ▼
 Members vote in EC elections → renew membership → cycle repeats
```

Key insight: **the free flagship event is the marketing engine** (draws the whole community, attracts sponsors), **membership is the trust/governance engine** (voting rights), and **ticketed events + sponsors are the profit engine**.

## 3. Common Website Feature Set (what these sites actually have)

- Home page with hero photos of past events + upcoming-event banner
- About / History / Mission
- Executive Committee page (photos + titles) — very important culturally
- Events calendar + past-event photo galleries
- Membership page: tiers, benefits, payment instructions (Zelle/PayPal) or online form
- Donate page (PayPal button, sometimes designated funds)
- Sponsors page / logo wall + sponsorship package PDF
- Constitution / bylaws page (PDF)
- Election Commission / election notices (bigger orgs)
- Contact form + Facebook page link (Facebook is often more active than the website)
- Souvenir magazine archive (PDFs)
