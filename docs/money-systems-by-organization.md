## Part A — General cultural associations, one by one

### Texas

**1. Bangladesh American Society of Greater Houston (BASGH)** — basgh.net
- 501(c)(3) since 2004, EIN 03-0531290, owns its facility ("BASGH Home", Sugar Land). FY2024 revenue $91,368 / net assets $619,283; a $282k spike year in 2022 ([990s](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/030531290)).
- Membership (Wix pricing plans, bought online): Annual Individual **$25**, Annual Family **$50**, Lifetime Individual **$150**, Lifetime Family **$250**.
- Payments: **Zelle to donate@basgh.org** (QR code), donation form with $10/$20/$50/$100 presets (one-time or monthly), and a dedicated **PayPal link for FOBANA 2027 fundraising** in the main nav.
- 12 events/year (concert, Pohela Boishakh, Pitha Mela + AGM, Iftar, Eid, food-bank volunteering, mobile consular service). Concert prices and sponsorship tiers not published. Has a public "Financials" file-share page — rare transparency gesture.

**2. Bengali Association of Dallas–Fort Worth (BADFW)** — badfw.org
- 501(c)(3) since 1992 (EIN 75-2434783). FY2024 revenue $210,478, 99.3% from program services, zero liabilities, volunteer officers ([990s](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/752434783)).
- **The cleanest modern money stack surveyed**: everything runs through **Zeffy** (zero-fee nonprofit platform) — donations and ticketing, with automatic tax receipts. No Zelle/PayPal at all.
- Durgotsav 2026 ticket prices (Zeffy): 3-day adult pass **$170** (6 meals included), visiting parent/teen $135, child $80, Thursday add-on $10. Early-bird sold out.
- Membership fees and sponsorship prices not published — both route to a contact form.

**3. Bangladeshi Expatriate Society of Texas (BEST)** — bestdfw.org
- 501(c)(3) since 2012 but files only 990-N postcards — **finances publicly invisible** despite running a headline mela.
- Membership: **$100 per 2-year term**; pay by **check or Zelle to (817) 303-9031 / BestDfwTx@gmail.com**.
- 14th Boishakhi Mela 2026 (Plano, headliner: MILES): **$20 general / $15 student / under-10 free**, sold via desieventsgo.com ("no service fee"). Picnic: $20/person, Zelle within 3 days or registration cancelled.
- Sponsorship: no tiers/prices — all negotiated directly with the chairman. Sponsors shown: Indopak Group, realtors, restaurants.
- Governance risk example: one person (chairman) is the contact for membership, sponsorship and payments.

**4. Bangladesh Association of Greater Austin (BAGA)** — bagatx.org
- 501(c)(3) since 2007; 990-N filer (under $50k/yr).
- **Membership is free** — join by adding yourself to the member database (Google Form). Donations via **PayPal.me/BAGATX**.
- Sponsorship tiers named (Platinum/Gold/Silver) but unpriced. Best-published governance of the Texas group: named board with Chair, Treasurer, Secretary, and recent elections.

**5. Bangladesh Association of North Texas (BANT)**
- Website down (expired SSL / hijacked-looking DNS); active on Facebook. 501(c)(3), EIN 75-2231803.
- 990s: FY2023 revenue **$335,712** (big-event year) but **negative net assets three years running** (−$17k in FY2025) — a real example of a community org overspending events.
- Boishakhi Mela 2026 (Frisco): **$10 online / $15 gate**.

### New York City

**6. Bangladesh Society Inc.** — bangladeshsocietyinc.com — *the election-economy model*
- Est. 1975, Elmhurst. Filing entity EIN 80-0508683, 501(c)(3) since 2015. FY2025 revenue $314,607 / net assets $491,492; **FY2018 (election year) spiked to $588,845** ([990s](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/800508683)).
- Membership: **General $10/year** (most buy 2 years/$20), **Life $500 (covers spouse too)**, plus Associate (non-voting) class. Membership forms double as **voter registration** — data "may be printed in the form of a voter list."
- Payments: paper forms accept cash/money order (general) and cash/card/check/**Zelle-QuickPay** (life); an online member portal takes card and Zelle. Donation form: $20–$500 presets, one-time/weekly/monthly/yearly/lifetime, designated to children's education, summer camps, clean water, community welfare.
- **The election money machine**: 19 executive posts contested every 2 years, run by a 7-member Election Commission with 5 borough voting centers. Candidate fees 2024: **$1,200 (member post) to $5,500 (president) + $500 materials package**. 2026 cycle: **35,321 paid voters** (candidate panels bulk-submit and often sponsor supporters' $20 fees); the Society projected **~$750,000 in election-cycle income**; election machinery itself costs $70k+ (52 voting machines, 55 polling officers, security).
- Assets: owns its office building; bought **210 graves** at Washington Memorial Park resold to members at cost — a signature member benefit.

**7. Bengali Club USA** — bengaliclubusa.org — *the sponsorship-spectacle model*
- 501(c)(3) since 2023 (EIN 88-3347106). Reported revenue only **$17k–$49k/yr** while staging Times Square Pohela Boishakh and Durga Puja — most sponsor value likely in-kind or through partners.
- Membership page 404s; payment subdomain (pay.bengaliclubusa.org) currently dead. Sponsorship tiers: Title ("Presented by [Your Brand]" on Times Square screens), Associate Platinum/Gold/Silver/Bronze, Prime Donor — **selling billboard visibility, not community access**; prices by contact only.

**8. Bangladeshi American Cultural Association (BACA)** — bacaus.com (Bronx)
- 501(c)(3), EIN 47-2065216. **$20/yr general, $200 lifetime** (verified via directory; the org's own site publishes no membership page — joining happens in person/by phone). 990-EZ scale: $7k–$20k. Runs the annual Bangla Mela, carrom tournaments, iftar.

**9. Jackson Heights Bangladeshi Business Association (JBBA)** — jbbany.org
- Business-association dues model: **General $60/yr (voting), Associate $90/yr, Life member = honorary non-voting class** (inverted from cultural orgs). Dues by mailed check or PayPal/card online. 35-member executive committee with contested elections; treasurer is a named business owner. No public 990.

**10. BACDYS** — bacdys.org (Brooklyn) — *the grant-funded service model*
- 501(c)(3), FY2024 revenue **$861,233** (94–100% grants/contributions every year), 19 employees. Named funders: New York Community Trust $225k, Make the Road New York $125k subgrant, CACF $42k; participates in Medicaid-linked WholeYouNYC referral network. No membership at all. Donations via **Zeffy** + GoFundMe Charity.

**11. BDesh Foundation** — bdeshfoundation.org (Sterling, VA) — *the diaspora charity model*
- 501(c)(3) since 2010, **~$1.1–1.5M revenue/yr**, net assets $3.68M, all-volunteer ([990s](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/263497451)).
- Collects: own online portal, mailed checks, the **FeelingBlessed** Muslim-giving app, Zelle via partner campaigns. Designated channels: **general, Zakat, Sadaqa, Qurbani**; e.g., "$75 funds an eye surgery."
- Disburses through **vetted registered NGOs in Bangladesh** (screened against the State Department database) — the compliant way to move US charity money to Bangladesh.

**14. Poorba (Mountain House, Bay Area)** — poorba.us

- NABC 2026 (White Plains, NY) revenue architecture:
  - Registration: **Single $250 / Couple $400 / Student $150 / Kid $50**, 10% early-bird window, hard refund cutoffs.
  - Add-ons: separately ticketed Kumar Sanu & Vishal-Shekhar concerts, NABC Idol entry fees, paid food registration, hotel blocks ($179–259/night).
  - Sponsorship = bundled hospitality: **Benefactor $1,200 → Bronze $3,500 (includes 4 hotel nights) → Silver $5,000 → Gold $10,000 (stage felicitation) → Platinum $25,000 (5-minute business speech slot) → Diamond $50,000** (8 badges, suite, top billing). Plus a paid exhibitor pavilion.

## Part B — Other organization types (the brainstorm)

**19. BMANA — Bangladesh Medical Association of North America** — bmana.org
- 501(c)(3) since 1982; national body ~$210–240k/yr plus separately incorporated state chapters, each with its own EIN.
- Dues: **Life $500, Active $150/2yr, Associate $100/2yr, Adjunct free** — gated by physician license/NPI.
- Convention is the engine: **registration $450** (with CME credits + gala), $300 non-practicing, $125 gala-only; **sponsors Gold $2k / Platinum $3k / Grand $5k** with hotel + souvenir-ad bundles. Payment by check/bank transfer via treasurer@bmana.org.
- Lesson: credentialed identity supports 10× higher dues than cultural orgs.

**20. AABEA — Bangladeshi Engineers & Architects** — aabea.org
- 501(c)(3), ~12 chapters. Revenue **doubles in even years** ($199k in 2024 vs $87k in 2023) — the biennial convention pulse: bank in convention years, spend down between. Dues unpublished; chapters insist on one official Google Form payment link ("do NOT use any other link") — implying impostor-link fraud has happened.

**21. District ("desher bari") associations**
- **Jalalabad Association of America (Sylhet)** — a **501(c)(7) social club**, so donations are NOT tax-deductible; genuinely a members' pool. ~$93k revenue, **$331k net assets** (~3.5× revenue — a community savings account). Funds iftars, picnics, World Sylhet Convention, scholarships, disaster relief.
- **Chittagong Association of North America** — 501(c)(3), **$24/2yr, $500 life**, referral required. Lives off an investment corpus ($200k assets, revenue now mostly investment income) between crisis-relief surges; delivered flood-relief cheques via the Bangladesh Consul General — relief money moves home through official/consular channels.
- **Greater Noakhali Society USA** — the endgame: **$661k revenue incl. rental real estate, $1.45M net assets** — converted community money into an income-producing building.

**22. Alumni associations**
- **BUETANA** — **zero dues** ("reasonable donations expected"), check + PayPal, 990-N scale; money flows back to BUET campus scholarships in Bangladesh.
- **Dhaka University Alumni Association USA** — **$20/2yr with Stripe checkout** (most modern payment rail found); reunion dinners priced to break even. Alumni orgs are the smallest-money type — prestige, not fundraising, is the point.

**23. Mosques serving Bangladeshi communities** (e.g., Jamaica Muslim Center / Masjid Al-Mamoor, Queens, est. 1976)
- Church-classified 501(c)(3) → **files no Form 990 at all; zero public financials**.
- Revenue calendar: weekly **Jummah cash collections**; a massive **Ramadan surge** (zakat = religiously obligated 2.5% of wealth, plus fitra and iftar sponsorships); capital **building-fund campaigns** (JMC used GoFundMe). Online rails: **Zeffy + PayPal** (peers use Zelle/Venmo/Cash App too). Membership exists but fees unpublished.
- The least transparent, most cash-heavy, and largest-capital-campaign type.

**24. University student associations (BSAs)** (e.g., ASU BSA, 138 members)
- Registered student orgs — no EIN, no 990. Funded by **university student-fee allocations**, cultural-night tickets (~$15, Penn State), and university-brokered bank sponsorships. Money sits in university-controlled accounts; surpluses are usually donated. The talent pipeline for every other org type.

**25. Community media** (Thikana $1.50/copy weekly; Bangla Patrika ~$20/col-inch ads; TBN24 24/7 TV)
- For-profit, ad-funded by the community's professional class (realtors, immigration lawyers, accident attorneys, political campaigns). TBN24: 20 staff in Queens + 120 in Dhaka, and ad revenue still doesn't cover costs — **owners subsidize it monthly**. Media monetizes the community rather than pooling from it, and is where orgs buy their event publicity.

## Part C — The actual process: what happens when someone pays

Three process patterns cover essentially every org surveyed.

### Pattern 1 — Manual flow: form + Zelle + treasurer spreadsheet (most common)
Used by Saikat, BAGA Atlanta, BEST, BCS Cleveland, and most orgs.
1. **Form first**: person fills a Google Form / website form (name, family members, email, phone, tier).
2. **Pay separately**: Zelle to the org email (e.g., accounts@saikat.org) with **name + purpose in the memo** ("Rahim Uddin — Family Membership 2026"); or cash/check handed to a committee member. BEST auto-cancels registrations if Zelle doesn't arrive within 3 days.
3. **Treasurer matches** the Zelle notification to the form entry (why the form-first rule exists — an unlabeled payment is unidentifiable).
4. **Record + confirm**: treasurer logs it in a member spreadsheet (name, tier, paid date, expiry) and confirms by email/WhatsApp. Some orgs issue a member ID card (Bangladesh Society requires a 2×2 photo; BAM issues cards) — shown at events and elections.
5. **At the event**: a door volunteer checks names against the member list (or card) → members free, others pay at the gate.
6. **Banking**: Zelle is linked to the org's bank account; treasurer keeps the ledger and reports at the AGM.

The entire "system" = one Google Form + one Zelle account + one spreadsheet + one volunteer with the list at the door.

### Pattern 2 — Platform flow: Zeffy / Square / Stripe (modern minority)
Used by BADFW, BACDYS, Jamaica Muslim Center, DUAAUSA.
1. "Become a member" / "Buy tickets" button → Zeffy/Square/Stripe checkout.
2. Card payment → instant email receipt/e-ticket (Zeffy auto-generates the tax-deduction receipt).
3. The platform builds the member/attendee list automatically — treasurer exports it; no memo-matching.
4. Event check-in from the platform's list or ticket scan; platform deposits to the org bank account on schedule.

### Pattern 3 — At-the-door flow: cash at the gate (melas, one-off events)
Used by BANT, BEST, and most melas.
1. Online presale cheaper ($10) than the gate ($15) to push people online.
2. Gate: cash box run by 2–3 trusted volunteers, wristband/hand-stamp entry; members and small kids free on card/list.
3. Vendors pay booth fees the same way — negotiated by phone beforehand, Zelle or cash on the day.
4. After the event, volunteers **count cash together** (never one person alone), treasurer deposits, and the event P&L goes into the AGM report.

### What the "Make Payment" button actually does — click-by-click, per org
1. **Zeffy (BADFW tickets)**: button → redirect to a zeffy.com page with the org's banner → pick ticket quantities → attendee names + email → card fields on the same page (plus an optional-tip-to-Zeffy dropdown, settable to $0) → instant e-ticket + tax receipt by email.
2. **Square storefront (BAGA Atlanta membership)**: button → bagamembers.square.site, tiers displayed like shop products → add to cart → Square card checkout → retail-style email receipt; member list comes from Square's export.
3. **PayPal**: (a) embedded smart button (Poorba) → popup → PayPal login or guest card → thank-you on page; (b) hosted payment link (BASGH FOBANA fund, BAGA Austin's PayPal.me) → paypal.com page, amount box → PayPal receipt. The org's site never changes; they watch the PayPal dashboard.
4. **Wix member plans (BASGH membership)**: Buy Now → create an account on the org's site → on-site Wix checkout → the buyer becomes a logged-in site member with the plan on their profile.
5. **Third-party desi ticket marketplaces (BEST via desieventsgo, Poorba via Sulekha)**: redirect to the marketplace's event page → card checkout there → e-ticket; marketplace pays the org out later.
6. **No checkout at all**:
   - **Zelle instructions page (Saikat, BCS Cleveland, BEST picnic)**: the "pay" page is just text + QR code ("Zelle to accounts@saikat.org, name + event in memo"). Payment happens in the payer's own banking app; confirmation is manual (email/WhatsApp from the treasurer, sometimes days later).
   - **In-house card form (CAB/NABC)**: raw card fields built into the org's own site by its web vendor — functional but trust-killing; the anti-pattern to avoid.

Spectrum: **instant-confirmation** (Zeffy/Square/Stripe/PayPal — immediate ticket, clean list for the org) vs **trust-based** (Zelle/cash — zero fees, manual reconciliation, delayed confirmation). Most orgs run both side by side.

### Behind the scenes — from payment to the org actually receiving it

**Setup that must exist first**: incorporate in the state → IRS EIN → 501(c)(3) determination → **bank account in the org's name** (needs EIN + incorporation docs + board resolution naming signers, usually president + treasurer). Every rail attaches to that account; Zelle to a personal Gmail/phone (the BEST pattern) means money lands in someone's personal account — commingling, the root of most disputes.

**How each rail delivers the money:**
| Rail | Where money sits | Fee | Arrives in org bank | Gotchas |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zelle | goes straight to org bank | $0 | minutes | irreversible; no refunds/chargebacks; memo-matching is manual |
| PayPal | org's **PayPal balance** first | ~1.99%+$0.49 (nonprofit) | after manual withdrawal (~1 day) | account freezes on new/spiky volume; chargebacks possible |
| Zeffy | processor → ACH payout | $0 (payer tips fund Zeffy) | scheduled/monthly payout | dashboard auto-builds donor list + tax receipts |
| Square / Stripe | processor → auto payout | ~2.6–2.9% + fixed | next day / ~2 days | dashboard is the bookkeeping |
| Ticket marketplace | **marketplace holds everything until after the event** | commission | ~5–7 days post-event | org fronts all event costs; sponsor money is the working capital |
| Cash / checks | gate cash box / mail | $0 | on deposit | count with 2–3 people + signed sheet; checks payable to org name only |

**Inside the org once money lands**: treasurer's ledger (spreadsheet or QuickBooks) tags every dollar in (dues/tickets/sponsors/donations/vendor fees) and attaches receipts to every dollar out → monthly reconciliation against the bank statement → controls: two signatures over a threshold, treasurer ≠ president, cash never counted alone → $250+ donors get written acknowledgment letters (IRS requirement for their deduction) → year totals become the AGM treasurer's report → same numbers become the IRS filing (990-N under $50k, 990-EZ under $200k, full 990 above — why BADFW/Saikat finances are public and BEST's aren't).

**The handover**: after each election, bank signatory rights are formally transferred to the new treasurer at the branch. Refusing the handover is how orgs split into rival factions with duplicate websites (see FOBANA).

### The reverse flow — expenses
Volunteers spend their own money on food/decor/supplies → submit receipts → treasurer reimburses from the org account (bylaws often require two signatures on checks above a threshold). The receipts-in/receipts-out ledger becomes the annual financial report presented at the AGM.

## Part D — What all this teaches Utah USA

1. **Zelle-to-a-named-org-email + a matching Google Form is the community standard** (Saikat accounts@, BAGA ec_treasurer@, CAB, BASGH donate@). Always pair the payment with a form so the treasurer can reconcile. Publish the fee math like Saikat does (PayPal 1.99%+$0.49 vs Zelle free) to steer payers.
2. **Zeffy is the modern upgrade** (BADFW, BACDYS, Jamaica Muslim Center): zero platform fees, automatic tax receipts, handles both donations and ticketing — the best of both worlds for a new org.
3. **Membership = access + franchise.** Two viable pricing philosophies: cheap-and-mass ($10–50/yr; NYC/Texas model, where the vote drives volume) vs. premium-bundle ($180–650/yr; Saikat/BAGA/Poorba model, where dues prepay event admission + meals). Calendar-year terms with a hard mid-year cutoff (Aug 15/31) and lifetime tiers ($150–500) for upfront capital.
4. **Sponsorship is where the real event money is, and it's almost always negotiated offline.** Only Poorba and the big conventions publish price cards ($800–$5,000 locally; $1,200–$50,000 at convention scale). Sponsor benefits that work: souvenir-book ads, stage announcements, booths, Times-Square-style visibility, and (at conventions) bundled hotel + badges.
5. **Non-member event products matter**: Poorba's $50 "Buddy" weekend pass, BEST's $15–20 mela tickets, BADFW's $170 meal-inclusive puja pass — priced access for people who won't buy membership.
6. **Transparency is the differentiator.** Most orgs publish nothing (990-N postcards, "contact us" pricing, payments to personal Gmail/phone numbers, checks to volunteers' homes); BANT shows what overspending looks like (negative net assets), BAFOCF shows status decay. A public financials page (BASGH), full 990 filing (BAGA Atlanta, BADFW), and a named treasurer inbox are cheap trust-builders a new org can adopt from day one.
7. **Designated funds raise more**: Bangladesh Society (education/camps/water/welfare), BDesh (Zakat/Sadaqa/Qurbani, "$75 = one eye surgery"), BAG (scholarship fund) — giving donors a specific purpose beats a generic donate button.
8. **Watch the failure modes**: rival factions running duplicate websites (FOBANA), election-time bulk membership buying (Bangladesh Society), payment-link impostors (AABEA's warning), dead payment domains (Bengali Club USA), hijacked DNS (BANT), single-person money handling (BEST). Website + bylaws + treasurer-controlled rails are the defenses.
